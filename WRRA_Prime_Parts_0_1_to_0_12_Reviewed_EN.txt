2. Actual spatial calculation and correction
Freeze the 0.6 source population at t=180 s. Place Q=64,617 quartet sites in a line ten light-seconds apart. A photon emitted in the +x direction (one of six equally weighted axes) from site i may reach deuteron target i+1; the last site has no next target. Assign independent neighboring fusion and deuteron marginals F/Q and D/Q. Perform ten Bernoulli contact attempts with q=.1. Thus the expected absorbed first-generation fusion photons are F × (Q−1)/Q × (1/6) × (D/Q) × [1−(1−q)^10] = 134.812677492537, where D=C−2F=1,266.881572721. The photon pulse has ten seconds of flight and a finite contact window after the frozen source snapshot; target cooling, beta and recapture then evolve for 180 s. Simultaneous source production and radiation transport are not claimed.
The review caught an energy bookkeeping mistake during construction of 0.12: adding `initial_beta_budget_MeV` to the already recorded electron plus antineutrino energy would count 680,618.461596 MeV twice. The corrected code asserts absolute closure to the inherited source, 958,526,427.960422 MeV, before and after target feedback. Around 134.81 fusion photons are absorbed and around 133.48 new capture photons are retained; the photon pool changes from 1,790,199.616097 to 1,787,291.781293 MeV with total energy conserved. This is a correction of this release's construction audit, not a retrospective change to measured data.

3. Scope and reproducibility
Neighboring site independence, a pulse after the t=180 s source snapshot, ten-light-second geometry, q and cooling are stated choices. Prime labels mark provenance, not coordinates. Photons move at the fixed light speed; nuclei are not transported ten light-seconds. Secondary capture photons remain in the energy ledger but are not routed to another neighbor. Microscopic cross sections, correlated site microstates, simultaneous spacetime evolution and multi-generation absorption have not been computed in this release.
